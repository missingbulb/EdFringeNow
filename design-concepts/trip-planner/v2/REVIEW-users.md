# Review — simulated first-use session

Priya (41, first Fringe trip with partner and kids 6 and 9, budget-conscious), Tom & Alex (30s couple, comedy fans, hate forms), Margaret (68, alone, International + Book Festival, reads everything, dislikes clutter) walked all twelve screens cold on a laptop. One markup fact applies to all three: no direction gives its answer tokens a pointer, title or role, so "you can reopen this" is purely visual everywhere.

## Postcard
1. Yes — the postmark carrying the dates and the "Stick on…" sheet. Breaks on 03: the postcard's back is 21 stubs at 10–11px grey. Margaret: "there's a lot on this page."
2. Best transition (wallet → "Thrifty" sticker), but the strip mixes pills, stamps and rounds, "EIF" is an abbreviation none of them know, and "Southside" is a token for a question nobody answered. Priya: "what's Southside? I didn't pick that."
3. Party, wallet, sprinter, train, guesthouse: yes. Fail: "City too" (Priya: "city what?"), a sun for "Season", "Name" (of what?), and every correction sticker needs its caption.
4. Too much. Worst: the stubs' second line at 11px, plus two pen-note instructions.
5. Nobody found "peel" without the pen note; Margaret: "do I drag it?" Once open, the five stickers read fine except scope — Priya: "No comedy… for the whole trip?" The Tattoo's single "Not for me" all three read correctly.
6. (a) Stub meta ≥13px, one line. (b) Curled corner on every stub, peel = click, delete the pen notes. (c) "Southside" → "Guesthouse"; spell out EIF.

## Journey map
1. Partly. Makes it: the route, and the tinted picture per day. Breaks it: Arrive/Leave boxes, chips and the unlabelled 31-box strip on 01. Tom: "it's a booking site with hills."
2. Most legible mechanics: the answered stop wears the answer's picture and names it, and the route shows what's left. Priya: "only five stops, good." The check badge reads as "done", so reopening isn't obvious.
3. Party, train, guesthouse: yes. Fail: the greyed future-stop icons, "1×" (Priya: "one times what?"), the lyre (Margaret: "a harp?").
4. Right — but the worst offender is text that is present and unreadable: five stubs end in "…".
5. Found: stubs look like cards, all three clicked one. Options are clear; two labels hurt — "Not now" (Tom: "as in later?") and "No kids' shows", where Priya froze: "I meant not this one." "Not interested / Keep it" on the Tattoo is the cleanest one-off of the three.
6. (a) Number the boxes or drop the strip. (b) No ellipses. (c) "Not now" → "Not this time"; "No kids' shows" → "Fewer kids' shows"; "1×" → a word.

## One big question
1. Yes, loudest — suitcase → moons → waving hand, then "Here's your trip!" with confetti. Tom/Alex: "this is fun." Breaks: money is nowhere. Priya: "how much is all this?" — her one give-up. Margaret: "a children's app," but she can read every word.
2. Best: the eight dashed circles on 01 announce the tokens before any exist, and each fills with the exact picture just tapped; by 03 all three knew what the row was. Still nothing says "click to change."
3. Party, wallet, sprinter, curtain, comedy face, Tattoo castle: yes. Fail: the moons (Margaret: "what are the moons for?"), the guitar for "International" (Priya: "international what?"), and one dash meaning both "free time" (Rest) and "unbooked" (Dishoom).
4. Right, verging on too little — the absent prices. Worst word: "Shuffle", no picture; Margaret: "I don't want it shuffled."
5. Long-press is undiscoverable on a laptop — Priya never opened a tile. Once flipped, "Not this… time / show / comedy / Pleasance" is the clearest wording of the three, and "One night only" over a single option explains itself.
6. (a) £ on every ticket tile and a total under the title. (b) Flip on click, with a curled corner as the tell. (c) Caption the moons "4 nights"; "International Festival" on the card.

## Ranking
1. One big question — the only one all three could operate end to end, and the only tokens that explain themselves.
2. Journey map — most legible and Margaret's choice, but 01 reads as the booking form the owner rejected.
3. Postcard — most charming opening, but 03 turns back into the dense grid at 10–11px, and the peel gesture is invisible.

## Steal into the winner
- Postcard's totals block (4 nights £528 / Train £464 / Tickets £312) — Priya's delight, missing entirely.
- Journey's "NOTHING BOOKED YET" pill — the reassurance Priya actually reacted to.
- Journey's click-the-stub fold-out, wearing Postcard's curled corner as the tell.
