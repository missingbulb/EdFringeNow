# Market & audience

The scale of the Edinburgh Fringe and the audience behaviour Fringe Discover is
built for — the "overwhelm" problem and the in-the-moment decision. Compiled
once, refined in place.

## Scale (2025 festival)

- **3,893 shows** across **301 venues**, **53,942 performances**, artists from
  **62 countries**.
- **~2.6 million tickets** issued (2,604,404) — roughly flat on 2024, still below
  the 2019 pre-pandemic peak of about 3 million.

This scale is the product premise: the official programme is a catalogue that
rewards planning ahead and overwhelms the person deciding in the moment.

## The overwhelm / decision problem

- With more than 3,800 shows, choosing "can feel like a daunting task"; the
  standard advice is to lean on filters, star ratings and reviews, and to
  **embrace serendipity** — the show you'd never have booked is often the one
  people still talk about.
- Planning guidance repeatedly stresses **leaving time to travel between venues**
  and pacing the day — the very reachability constraint Fringe Discover makes
  first-class rather than an afterthought.

## Audience segments

From the product brief (`docs/product-spec.md`, an internal hypothesis not yet
externally validated): visitors mid-festival on foot without a fixed plan; people
with a _gap_ between commitments; locals and tourists intimidated by the
programme's size. Decisive in the moment rather than researchers, with an
emotional target of "delight + relief." These segments are design assumptions —
validating them against real behavioural data is an open question below.
